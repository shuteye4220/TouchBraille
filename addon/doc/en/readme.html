Touch Braille Keyboard

Type braille on a touchscreen by tapping finger chords, using NVDA's own braille input. Your input table and contracted or uncontracted setting come from NVDA's Braille settings.

Turning it on and off

  • Five-finger double tap turns the keyboard on or off.
  • Or three-finger tap to cycle touch modes until you hear "braille keyboard mode".
  • Two-finger swipe down turns it off from inside the keyboard.

While the keyboard is on, all touches go to it; other NVDA touch gestures don't work until you turn it off.

Layout (tabletop, screen facing up)

  • 6 dot, left to right: dots 3, 2, 1, 4, 5, 6.
  • 8 dot, left to right: dots 7, 3, 2, 1, 4, 5, 6, 8.
  • Space bar: a strip along the bottom edge (nearest you). Tap it with a thumb for space, or hold it with dots for a space chord such as space with dot 1. On by default for the 8 dot keyboard.

As on a braille display, dot 7 alone erases and dot 8 alone is enter.

Swipes

  • One finger right: space.
  • Two fingers right: enter.
  • One finger left: backspace.
  • Two fingers left: delete previous word.
  • Two fingers up: calibrate.
  • Two fingers down: turn the keyboard off.

Calibrating

Swipe up with two fingers, then place all 6 or 8 fingers where they rest naturally and lift them. Calibration is saved separately for the 6 and 8 dot keyboards. Until you calibrate, the dots are spread evenly across the screen.

Space chord commands

Space chords are standard NVDA braille keyboard gestures, named like "bk:space+dot1". Assign them in NVDA's Input Gestures dialog: choose a command, press Add, then type the chord. Bindings you make also apply to a braille display's keyboard.

Settings

NVDA menu, Preferences, Settings, Touch Braille: choose 6 or 8 dots, swipe distance, where the space bar is used and how tall it is, and reset calibration.
